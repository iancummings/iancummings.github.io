3. IOLs

intermediate = arm’s legth

Biometer? Visual axis, not just pupil centre.

monocular/monofocal - choose focal length, need reading glasses (varifocal possible?) clear night vision, etc no focus closer than 1m

multifocal/trifocal - distance at top, central circle intermediate, bottom near vision contrast loss, night driving halos, glare risk. Also requires precise positioning, centration and for astigmatism the rotation matters too - doesn’t sound good to me.

EDOF - like Fresnel lenses? very small risk of glare, halos or starburst (puresee). Distance and near vision - need readers for close work. Slight loss of contrast for beam shaping lenses (20%), loss of light for pinhole versions. Diffractive produces halo/glare. can use slightly different prescriptions to give a slightly wider range. focus down to 60cm?

monovision - two monofocal lenses of different focal lengths; one eye near, one eye far. less halo risk, but some patients cannot adjust well and report weaker depth perception, distracting blur during night time driving. Can test drive this first with contact lenses.

toric lens - for astigmatism which causes blur across all vision distances. If not included then the result of cataract surgey is likely to be disappointing. Does require precise rotational positioning though.

4. Cataract Surgery

4.1. Post Operative Experience

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GhkYzzjLg3w

  • eye drops; steroid, antibiotic, analgesic, artificial tears

  • depth perception a little off; stairs, kerbs, etc. Pop out lens from eye

  • dryness, irritation, redness, foreign body sensation for a few weeks

  • first few days; avoid heavy lifting, bending over

  • 2-4 weeks; no strenuous activity

  • subconjunctival haemorrhage possible; days/weeks healing.

  • brain needs to adapt to new colours, focussing etc for 4 weeks

  • 1 month - swelling, inflammation should have all settled from halos, glare, dry eyes, light sensi, blue, glare, halos

  • sudden drop-offs; acuity, extreme blur. Some variation expected, but sudden drops aren’t.

  • secondary cataract - posterior capsular opacification; 20-50% occurance - quite common.
    Fixed by laser to make a hole in the back of the capsule.

  • Any action that produces the Valsalva manuever (increasing the thoracic pressure such as bearing down when moving your bowels) will increase your intraocular pressure - which is definitely a big no-no immediately after surgery. see more [1]

How It Works

(The Four Phases)

Phase 1 (Strain Start): Pressure jumps in the chest, squeezing the aorta and causing a brief rise in blood pressure.

Phase 2 (Sustained Strain): Blood flow returning to the heart drops because high chest pressure blocks veins. Blood pressure falls, and the heart rate speeds up to compensate.

Phase 3 (Release): Pressure in the chest drops when breathing resumes, causing a short dip in blood pressure.

Phase 4 (Recovery): Blood rushes back into the heart, causing blood pressure to briefly spike above normal before slowing the heart rate back to baseline.

Common Causes and UsesEveryday activities: Occurs naturally during heavy weightlifting, coughing, or straining during a bowel movement.Medical treatments: Doctors use a controlled Valsalva Maneuver to help reset abnormal rapid heart rhythms like supraventricular tachycardia (SVT).Equalizing pressure: Used by divers or flyers to pop their ears and balance middle ear pressure.

  • AI Overview
    How soon after cataract surgery can I do strenuous exercise?

For most people, it is best to wait 3 to 4 weeks before doing heavy lifting or strenuous exercise after cataract surgery.

Exercise Recovery Timeline

    • Day 1: You can do light activities like short, gentle walks. Avoid raising your heart rate, bending over, or lifting things.

    • Week 1: You can do slow stretching (without bending too far forward) and light indoor movement.

    • Weeks 1 to 2: You can try light gym work, golf, or tennis. Wait 2 weeks for yoga or Pilates movements that require bending down.

    • Weeks 3 to 4: You can slowly return to more strenuous exercise and heavy lifting, provided your doctor agrees.

    • Weeks 4 to 6: You can usually return to swimming (with goggles), running, and heavy weightlifting after final clearance from your eye surgeon. [1, 2, 3, 4]

Strenuous activity raises the pressure inside your eye. This extra pressure can harm the healing incision or shift the new lens.
